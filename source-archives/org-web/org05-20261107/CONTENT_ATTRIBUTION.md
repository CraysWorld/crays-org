# Attributing CRAYS-original prose

The prose identified in [CONTENT_LICENSE.md](CONTENT_LICENSE.md) and [content-license-scope.json](content-license-scope.json) is licensed by CRAYS AG under [CC BY-SA 4.0 International](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). The [unchanged legal code](LICENSE-CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt) controls; this guide adds no license condition.

## A practical attribution

Use the original title when one is supplied, name **CRAYS AG**, link the original material where reasonably practicable, identify CC BY-SA 4.0 and describe changes. For example, fill in the placeholders:

> Original title, if supplied — CRAYS AG. Source: original source URL. Licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Changes: describe the changes, or state that the text is unchanged.

If no title is supplied, omit that field rather than inventing a title or author year. The scope manifest provides immutable source-container URLs when a separate original page URL is not available. A container URL identifies the source version; it does not license the container's code, media or other excluded material.

Retain any creator identification, copyright, attribution, license and warranty-disclaimer notices supplied with the licensed prose, along with notices of previous modifications, as required by the license. Indicate your own modifications. Attribution may be presented in any reasonable manner suited to the medium and context; the example above is not a mandatory format and does not replace supplied attribution.

## Sharing adaptations

When sharing an adaptation of the licensed prose, comply with the ShareAlike terms in Section 3(b) of the legal code and identify the applicable adapter's license. The original file hashes identify the source revision, not a restriction on making or redistributing adaptations. Do not imply CRAYS AG endorsement.

This guidance applies only to the licensed CRAYS-original prose. Code, registry metadata, images, fonts, logos, external resources and other excluded material need their own applicable terms. Historical legal-text pages remain historical texts, not approved current policies.
